# Style Guides — Reference for Editorial and Citation Conventions

A style guide is a standardised set of editorial rules: how to format dates, numbers, headings, lists, quotations, and citations; whether to use the Oxford comma, the em-dash, sentence case or title case; how to abbreviate; how to render a reference. Choosing the right one is not a matter of taste — it is dictated by the audience (journal, publisher, employer, jurisdiction). This skill is a fast-reference card for the eight style guides the writer agent meets most often, plus the French typographic rules that apply across all French-language output.

The writer agent must (1) identify the required style from the user request or the target outlet, (2) apply the corresponding rules consistently across the whole document, and (3) flag conflicts when the user mixes house styles (e.g., "APA citations but Chicago footnotes").

---

## When to Apply

Apply a specific style guide when the request involves any of the following:

- An academic submission to a journal, conference, or thesis committee that names a style.
- A book or chapter for a publisher that imposes a manuscript spec (Chicago, MLA).
- A news or feature article for a publication that follows AP, Reuters, or a Le Monde-style house guide.
- A medical document, regulatory submission, or clinical guideline (AMA, Vancouver, ICMJE).
- An engineering or computer-science paper (IEEE, ACM).
- A Brazilian academic work (ABNT NBR 6023, 14724, 10520).
- Any French-language document — French typographic rules apply by default regardless of the citation style chosen.

Do NOT apply a specific guide when:

- The outlet has its own explicit instructions — those override any general guide.
- The text is internal communication with no external audience — apply the team or company style book if one exists.
- The user wants a quick answer and has not specified a style — pick a sensible default (Chicago for humanities, APA for sciences, AP for journalism) and note the choice.

When two guides conflict, the outlet's instructions to authors win; then the more specific guide (journal-specific) over the more general (APA generic); then the most recent edition.

## Per-Guide Reference

### APA 7 (American Psychological Association, 7th ed., 2020)

- **Scope.** Psychology, education, social sciences, nursing, communication.
- **Date format.** Long: "January 5, 2026". Short (tables, references): "2026-01-05" not used; instead "(2026, January 5)" inside parentheticals.
- **Number format.** Words for 0-9 in prose; numerals for 10 and above; numerals for all measurements, percentages, statistics. Use commas for thousands ("1,000"; "1,500,000"). Use "1.5 million" rather than "1,500,000" only in non-technical prose.
- **Heading capitalization.** Title case for level 1-2, sentence case in references. Title case capitalises all major words; articles, short prepositions, and conjunctions stay lowercase unless first.
- **Oxford comma.** Required. "A, B, and C".
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash (—) for parenthetical breaks, set tight (no spaces). En-dash (–) for ranges ("pp. 10–25") and compound modifiers ("New York–London flight"). Hyphen (-) for compound words.
- **Quotation marks.** Double "..." for direct quotes; single '...' inside. Periods and commas go inside the closing quote (US convention).
- **In-text citation.** Author-date: "(Smith, 2023)" or "Smith (2023) argued that..." with page number for direct quotes: "(Smith, 2023, p. 45)".
- **Reference example (journal article).**
  > Smith, J. A., & Doe, R. B. (2023). The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. *Journal of Cognitive Science*, *45*(3), 210-225. https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045
- **Italics.** Journal titles, book titles, volume numbers in references; technical terms on first use.
- **DOI.** Always include as a clickable URL.

### Chicago Manual of Style 17 / Turabian 9 (2017 / 2018)

- **Scope.** Humanities (history, literature, art history), books, university press monographs, theses. Two systems: Notes-Bibliography (humanities) and Author-Date (sciences).
- **Date format.** "5 January 2026" (UK convention) or "January 5, 2026" (US); pick one and stick to it.
- **Number format.** Words for whole numbers under 101 ("ninety-eight" then "101"); numerals from 101 up. Numerals always for measurements and percentages. Commas for thousands.
- **Heading capitalization.** Headline (title) case for all levels.
- **Oxford comma.** Required.
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash (—) tight, no spaces. En-dash for ranges and to indicate "to" between equals ("Boston–New York rivalry"). Hyphen for compounds.
- **Quotation marks.** Double for primary quotes, single nested. Punctuation inside the closing quote.
- **Citation format (Notes-Bibliography).** Footnote on first cite, shortened on subsequent. Full bibliographic entry in the bibliography.
- **Reference example (journal article, bibliography).**
  > Smith, John A., and Robert B. Doe. "The Effect of Sleep on Memory Consolidation." *Journal of Cognitive Science* 45, no. 3 (2023): 210–25. https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.
- **Footnote example.**
  > 1. John A. Smith and Robert B. Doe, "The Effect of Sleep on Memory Consolidation," *Journal of Cognitive Science* 45, no. 3 (2023): 210, https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.
- **Bibliography sort.** Alphabetical by first author's surname.

### MLA 9 (Modern Language Association, 9th ed., 2021)

- **Scope.** Humanities literature, languages, cultural studies, comparative literature.
- **Date format.** Day-month-year: "5 January 2026" or abbreviated "5 Jan. 2026".
- **Number format.** Spell out one-word numbers ("one", "twenty", "one hundred"); numerals for the rest. Numerals always for dates, addresses, measurements.
- **Heading capitalization.** Title case throughout.
- **Oxford comma.** Recommended but not strictly required; pick one and be consistent.
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash tight; en-dash for ranges; hyphen for compounds.
- **Quotation marks.** Double for primary, single nested. Punctuation inside the closing quote.
- **In-text citation.** Author-page, no comma, no year: "(Smith 210)". For multi-author: "(Smith and Doe 210)".
- **Works Cited example (journal article).**
  > Smith, John A., and Robert B. Doe. "The Effect of Sleep on Memory Consolidation." *Journal of Cognitive Science*, vol. 45, no. 3, 2023, pp. 210-25, https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.
- **MLA 9 "core elements."** Author. Title of source. Title of container, Other contributors, Version, Number, Publisher, Publication date, Location. Apply in order, separated by periods or commas.

### AP (Associated Press Stylebook, current edition)

- **Scope.** US journalism, newswire, public relations, corporate communications.
- **Date format.** Month-day-year with abbreviated month if used with a specific date: "Jan. 5, 2026"; spell out March, April, May, June, July when standing alone or with a year only.
- **Number format.** Spell out zero through nine; numerals for 10 and above. Always numerals for ages, percentages ("5%"), money ("$5"), and dimensions. Use "1 million" rather than "1,000,000" in prose.
- **Heading capitalization.** Title case for headlines, sentence case for subheads and decks.
- **Oxford comma.** Not used. "A, B and C".
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash with spaces on both sides: " — ". Hyphen for compound modifiers and ranges (AP avoids en-dashes).
- **Quotation marks.** Double for primary, single nested. Punctuation inside.
- **Citation format.** AP discourages footnotes and bibliographies in news copy; attribution is in-text: "the report said" or "according to a 2026 study in the Journal of Cognitive Science".
- **Attribution example.**
  > A 2023 study published in the Journal of Cognitive Science found that sleep affects memory consolidation, researchers John Smith and Robert Doe wrote.
- **AP datelines.** "WASHINGTON (AP) — ..." at the start of a story.
- **AP titles.** Abbreviate "Gov.", "Sen.", "Rep.", "Lt. Gov." before a name; spell out and lowercase when not directly before a name.

### AMA 11 (American Medical Association Manual of Style, 11th ed., 2020)

- **Scope.** Medicine, clinical research, JAMA, NEJM-aligned journals, regulatory submissions.
- **Date format.** "January 5, 2026" in prose; "2026" alone for year-only references.
- **Number format.** Numerals throughout, including 0-9. Spell out only when starting a sentence or in casual usage. Use SI units; comma not used for 4-digit numbers ("1000" not "1,000"); space-thin separator for 5+ digit numbers ("10 000").
- **Heading capitalization.** Sentence case for article titles in references; title case for the journal's own headings.
- **Oxford comma.** Required.
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash tight; en-dash for ranges; hyphen for compounds.
- **Quotation marks.** Double primary, single nested.
- **In-text citation.** Superscript numerals in citation order: "...as shown previously.¹" Multiple cites collapsed: "...several reports.²⁻⁴" Numbers placed after punctuation in US convention.
- **Reference example (journal article).**
  > 1. Smith JA, Doe RB. The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. *J Cogn Sci*. 2023;45(3):210-225. doi:10.1234/jcs.2023.0045
- **Author names.** Surname first, initials no periods, no "and" before the last author, comma-separated. List up to 6 authors; if 7+, list first 3 then "et al".
- **Journal abbreviation.** Use the NLM/PubMed standard abbreviation.

### IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers)

- **Scope.** Engineering, electrical engineering, computer science conference and journal papers (signal processing, telecom, robotics).
- **Date format.** "January 2026" or "Jan. 2026"; full date "5 Jan. 2026".
- **Number format.** Numerals for 10 and above; words for 0-9 unless followed by a unit ("3 V", "5 km"). Always numerals for measurements and equations.
- **Heading capitalization.** Title case for section titles ("II. Background and Related Work").
- **Oxford comma.** Recommended.
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash tight; en-dash for ranges; hyphen for compound modifiers.
- **Quotation marks.** Double primary, single nested.
- **In-text citation.** Bracketed numerals in citation order: "[1]" or "as shown in [1] and [3]". Numbers stay constant in the reference list; cite by number not author.
- **Reference example (journal article).**
  > [1] J. A. Smith and R. B. Doe, "The effect of sleep on memory consolidation," *J. Cogn. Sci.*, vol. 45, no. 3, pp. 210–225, Mar. 2023, doi: 10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.
- **Reference example (conference paper).**
  > [2] R. B. Doe, "Deep sleep models," in *Proc. IEEE Int. Conf. Cogn. Comput.*, San Francisco, CA, USA, 2023, pp. 100–105.
- **Journal abbreviation.** Use IEEE's official abbreviated form ("IEEE Trans. Signal Process.").

### Vancouver / ICMJE (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors)

- **Scope.** Biomedicine, NLM-indexed journals, WHO publications, Cochrane.
- **Date format.** "2026 Jan 5" in references; "5 January 2026" in prose.
- **Number format.** Numerals throughout; SI units; no commas in 4-digit numbers.
- **Heading capitalization.** Sentence case for titles in references.
- **Oxford comma.** Not strictly required, but commonly used.
- **Dash rules.** Em-dash sparingly; en-dash for ranges; hyphen for compounds.
- **Quotation marks.** Double primary, single nested.
- **In-text citation.** Parenthetical or superscript numerals in citation order: "(1)" or "¹". Reference list numbered in order of first appearance.
- **Reference example (journal article).**
  > 1. Smith JA, Doe RB. The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. J Cogn Sci. 2023 Mar;45(3):210-25. doi: 10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.
- **Author names.** Surname plus initials, no periods, up to 6 authors; "et al." after the 6th.
- **Journal abbreviation.** NLM Title Abbreviation list.

### ABNT NBR 6023 (Brazilian Association of Technical Standards, updated 2018)

- **Scope.** Brazilian academic theses, dissertations, monografias, articles in Brazilian journals.
- **Date format.** "5 jan. 2026" or "5 de janeiro de 2026" in prose.
- **Number format.** Comma as decimal separator ("3,14"); period or thin space as thousands separator ("1.000" or "1 000"). Numerals for measurements; words for 0-9 in prose otherwise.
- **Heading capitalization.** Sentence case for article titles in references; section headings follow the institution's manual (ABNT NBR 14724 for theses).
- **Oxford comma.** Not applicable to Portuguese syntax; standard list comma rules apply.
- **Dash rules.** Travessão (—) tight; hífen for compounds.
- **Quotation marks.** Double "..." or guillemets « ... » depending on house style.
- **In-text citation.** Author-date: "(SMITH, 2023)" with author in small caps when inside parentheses; "Smith (2023)" inside the sentence. Page number for quotes: "(SMITH, 2023, p. 45)".
- **Reference example (journal article).**
  > SMITH, J. A.; DOE, R. B. The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. *Journal of Cognitive Science*, [s. l.], v. 45, n. 3, p. 210-225, 2023. DOI: 10.1234/jcs.2023.0045. Disponível em: https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045. Acesso em: 5 jan. 2026.
- **Authors.** Surname in caps, then initials, separated by semicolon for multiple authors.
- **Place of publication.** Mandatory; use "[s. l.]" (sine loco) if unknown, "[s. n.]" (sine nomine) for unknown publisher.

## French Typographic Rules

French typography is enforced by the Imprimerie nationale (Lexique des règles typographiques en usage à l'Imprimerie nationale) and reflected in modern publishing manuals. These rules apply to every French-language document regardless of the citation style chosen.

### Espacement (non-breaking spaces)

| Sign | Space before | Space after | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| `:` | yes | yes | espace insécable avant |
| `;` | yes | yes | espace fine insécable avant |
| `!` | yes | yes | espace fine insécable avant |
| `?` | yes | yes | espace fine insécable avant |
| `%` | yes | yes | espace insécable avant |
| `,` | no | yes | espace sécable |
| `.` | no | yes | espace sécable |
| `«` (ouvrant) | yes | yes (insécable inside) | espace insécable après |
| `»` (fermant) | yes (insécable inside) | yes | espace insécable avant |
| `(` `[` | yes | no | espace sécable avant |
| `)` `]` | no | yes | espace sécable après |
| `–` (tiret demi-cadratin) | yes | yes | espace insécable des deux côtés |
| `—` (tiret cadratin) | yes | yes | espace insécable des deux côtés |
| `°` (degré) | no | yes | (e.g., `n°` collé) |

In Unicode, use U+202F NARROW NO-BREAK SPACE for the espaces fines (before `;`, `!`, `?`, `%`) and U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE for the espaces insécables (before `:`, inside guillemets, around tirets). When the renderer cannot guarantee fines, fall back to U+00A0 everywhere.

### Guillemets

- Use French guillemets « ... » for primary quotes.
- Use "..." or « ... » (recursive) for nested quotes; Le Monde uses "..." inside, the Imprimerie nationale uses « ... » recursively.
- Always an espace insécable after `«` and before `»`.
- Long quotes (≥ 4 lines): set in a block quote without guillemets, indented.

### Capitalisation et accents

- Capitales accentuées : ALWAYS accent capital letters in French ("École", "À bientôt", "Île-de-France"). This is mandatory per Académie française and ISO 9.
- Sigles : write without periods ("ONU", not "O.N.U."). Use full caps if pronounced letter by letter, mixed case if pronounced as a word ("Unicef", "Capes", "Cnrs" historically, though full caps is also accepted).
- Titres d'œuvres : italiques and capitalise only the first noun and any preceding articles/adjectives ("*La Recherche du temps perdu*", "*Le Petit Prince*").
- Mois et jours : lowercase ("lundi 5 janvier 2026", "le 14 juillet").
- Noms géographiques : capitalise the proper noun, lowercase the generic ("la mer Méditerranée", "le mont Blanc" but "le Mont-Blanc" as a place name).

### Nombres et unités

- Décimale : virgule ("3,14"), pas point.
- Milliers : espace fine insécable ("1 000 000") ; jamais de virgule.
- Heures : "14 h 30" avec espaces insécables, jamais "14:30" en typographie soignée (sauf horaires de transport).
- Dates : "5 janvier 2026" ou "le 5/01/2026".
- Pourcentages : "25 %" avec espace insécable avant le signe.
- Symboles d'unité SI : "10 km", "3 kg", "5 °C" (espace + °, puis lettre collée).

## Application Guidelines

1. **Identify the target.** Ask: "Which journal, publisher, or institution receives this?" Then match: APA for psychology, Chicago for humanities books, MLA for literature, AP for news, AMA for medicine, IEEE for engineering, Vancouver for biomedicine, ABNT for Brazilian academia.
2. **Read the instructions to authors.** Journals often modify the standard guide (e.g., "follow APA 7 but with numbered references"); the journal overrides the generic guide.
3. **Apply consistently.** Mixing two date formats in the same document is the most common copy-edit failure. Pick once, propagate everywhere.
4. **Use a reference manager.** Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote, or BibTeX with the right CSL (Citation Style Language) file applies the chosen style automatically. The writer should output references in CSL-JSON or BibTeX when many are involved.
5. **Localise to language.** A French APA paper still follows French typographic rules (espaces insécables, guillemets). Citation style and language style are orthogonal.
6. **Honour the language version.** APA has French-, Spanish-, and Portuguese-language adaptations; check whether one applies.
7. **When unsure, prefer Chicago Author-Date** for long-form scholarly prose and **APA 7** for empirical research papers. Both are widely accepted defaults.

## Templates / Sample Patterns

### APA 7 paragraph with citation

> Recent work has shown that sleep duration affects long-term memory (Smith & Doe, 2023). In a controlled trial of 120 participants, Smith and Doe (2023) reported that 8 hours of sleep increased recall accuracy by 15% relative to a 5-hour control (p = .003).

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography footnote

> Recent work has shown that sleep duration affects long-term memory.¹
>
> ---
> 1. John A. Smith and Robert B. Doe, "The Effect of Sleep on Memory Consolidation," *Journal of Cognitive Science* 45, no. 3 (2023): 210, https://doi.org/10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.

### IEEE numbered citation

> Sleep duration has been shown to affect long-term memory [1], with the strongest effect observed at the 7-9 h range [1], [2].
>
> ---
> [1] J. A. Smith and R. B. Doe, "The effect of sleep on memory consolidation," *J. Cogn. Sci.*, vol. 45, no. 3, pp. 210–225, Mar. 2023, doi: 10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.

### Vancouver superscript citation

> Sleep duration affects long-term memory.¹ Subsequent studies confirmed the effect.²⁻⁴
>
> ---
> 1. Smith JA, Doe RB. The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. J Cogn Sci. 2023 Mar;45(3):210-25.

### Exemple ABNT en portugais

> Estudos recentes demonstram que a duração do sono afeta a memória de longo prazo (SMITH; DOE, 2023, p. 215).
>
> ---
> SMITH, J. A.; DOE, R. B. The effect of sleep on memory consolidation. *Journal of Cognitive Science*, [s. l.], v. 45, n. 3, p. 210-225, 2023. DOI: 10.1234/jcs.2023.0045.

### Exemple en français (APA 7 adapté + typographie française)

> Des travaux récents ont montré que la durée du sommeil influence la consolidation mnésique (Smith & Doe, 2023). Dans un essai contrôlé portant sur 120 participants, Smith et Doe (2023) ont rapporté qu'une nuit de 8 h améliorait le rappel de 15 % par rapport à un groupe témoin de 5 h (p = 0,003).

Notice the espaces insécables avant `:`, `;`, `!`, `?`, `%`, et à l'intérieur des guillemets ; la virgule comme séparateur décimal ; et la capitale accentuée.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] The target style guide is identified and named at the top of the working notes.
- [ ] Instructions to authors (if any) have been consulted and override-conflicts noted.
- [ ] Dates use the chosen format consistently throughout (no mixing).
- [ ] Numbers follow the chosen cutoff and thousands-separator rule consistently.
- [ ] Headings are in the case prescribed by the guide.
- [ ] Oxford comma is used (or not) consistently.
- [ ] Dashes are correct: em-dash tight (APA, Chicago, MLA, AMA, IEEE) or spaced (AP).
- [ ] In-text citations match the guide and are reconciled with the reference list.
- [ ] Every in-text citation has a reference entry and vice versa.
- [ ] Reference entries are formatted exactly per the guide's example.
- [ ] DOI is present and clickable in every reference that has one.
- [ ] Journal abbreviations follow the official list (NLM for AMA/Vancouver, IEEE for IEEE).
- [ ] For French output: espaces insécables before `:`, `;`, `!`, `?`, `%`; guillemets « ... »; capitales accentuées; virgule décimale.
- [ ] For Portuguese output: ABNT punctuation, comma decimal, period thousand, [s. l.] / [s. n.] used when applicable.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mixing styles.** APA citations with Chicago footnotes, or Vancouver superscripts with author-date in-text. Pick one and propagate.
- **Forgetting the DOI.** APA 7 and AMA 11 require the DOI as a URL; omitting it is a flagged error.
- **Wrong en-dash / em-dash.** Using the hyphen "-" instead of "–" for ranges or "—" for breaks is a typographic mistake every editor catches.
- **Title case errors.** Capitalising short prepositions ("Of", "In", "On") or articles ("The", "A") mid-title in title-case headings is wrong. Lowercase them unless they are first or last.
- **AP comma + Oxford comma.** AP omits the Oxford comma; importing AP-styled copy into an APA document requires adding the serial comma back.
- **French espaces missing.** Writing "Bonjour:" instead of "Bonjour :" is a dead giveaway of mechanical translation. Insert U+202F or U+00A0.
- **French guillemets replaced by ASCII quotes.** Auto-conversion in editors often replaces « ... » with "..."; lock the typography setting.
- **ABNT capitals on author names lost.** ABNT requires SMITH, J. A. in references; lowercasing breaks the standard.
- **IEEE numbering reset.** IEEE numbers references in order of first citation; never re-sort the reference list alphabetically.
- **Vancouver "et al." too early.** Vancouver/ICMJE lists up to 6 authors before "et al."; APA 7 uses "et al." from 3+ authors in-text but lists up to 20 in the reference.
- **Forgetting place of publication in ABNT.** Mandatory; use "[s. l.]" if unknown, never omit.
- **Treating MLA 9 as MLA 8.** MLA 9 added container-based logic and changed publisher rules; ensure the right edition is applied.
- **Putting the period outside the quote in US English.** US convention is inside the closing quote; UK convention can be outside. APA, Chicago, MLA, AP, AMA all follow the US convention.
- **Capitales non accentuées en français.** Écrire "Etat" au lieu de "État" est une faute typographique, non un choix.
